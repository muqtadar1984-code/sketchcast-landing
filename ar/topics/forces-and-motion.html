الشرح

Introduction to Forces

A force is a push or a pull that acts upon an object as a result of interaction with another object. Forces are vector quantities, meaning they have both a magnitude, or size, and a direction. When forces act on an object, they can cause changes in the speed, shape, or direction of movement of that object. Forces are measured in units called newtons, named after the physicist Sir Isaac Newton, using an instrument called a newton meter or force meter.

In scientific diagrams, forces are represented by arrows. The direction of the arrow shows the direction in which the force is acting, and the length of the arrow typically represents the magnitude of the force. Multiple forces can act on a single object at the same time. The combined effect of all the forces acting on an object is known as the resultant force. Determining the resultant force involves adding forces that act in the same direction and subtracting forces that act in opposite directions.

Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

When two or more forces act on an object, their combined effect determines whether the motion of the object changes. If the opposing forces acting on an object are equal in size but opposite in direction, they are described as balanced forces. When forces are balanced, the resultant force acting on the object is zero newtons. According to the laws of motion, if an object is stationary and balanced forces act upon it, the object remains stationary. If the object is already moving at a constant speed in a straight line, it continues to move at that exact same speed and in that exact same direction.

Conversely, when the forces acting on an object are not equal in size, they are unbalanced forces. An unbalanced force results in a non-zero resultant force. When an unbalanced force acts on an object, it causes a change in the object's motion. This change can manifest as an increase in speed, a decrease in speed, or a change in the direction of travel. For example, when a cyclist pedals harder, the forward driving force becomes greater than the resistive forces of friction and air resistance, creating an unbalanced force that causes the bicycle to accelerate. If the cyclist stops pedalling, air resistance and friction become the dominant unbalanced forces, causing the bicycle to decelerate and eventually stop.

Turning Forces and Moments

Forces do not only cause objects to move in straight lines; they can also cause objects to rotate about a fixed point, pivot, or fulcrum. A turning force is known as a moment. The turning effect, or moment of a force, depends on two distinct factors: the size of the force applied and the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.

The magnitude of a moment is calculated using a specific mathematical relationship. The standard unit for a moment is the newton-metre, abbreviated as Nm. To increase the turning effect without increasing the applied force, the distance from the pivot can be increased. This principle explains why door handles are placed as far away from the door hinges as possible, making it much easier to open the heavy door with a relatively small push.

Calculating Moments

The calculation of a turning force follows a strict mathematical formula. The formula states that the moment equals the force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the pivot. In algebraic terms, this is expressed as moment equals force times distance. When using this formula in scientific calculations, the force must be measured in newtons, and the distance from the pivot must be measured in metres.

For a balanced system, such as a seesaw, the principle of moments applies. The principle of moments states that when an object is in equilibrium, the total clockwise moment about any pivot is equal to the total anticlockwise moment about that same pivot. This means a smaller force acting at a greater distance from the pivot can balance a larger force acting closer to the pivot, which is the foundational principle behind simple machines like levers, crowbars, and spanners.

المصطلحات الأساسية

Force
A push or a pull acting on an object, possessing both magnitude and direction.
Resultant force
The single overall force that represents the sum of all forces acting on an object.
Balanced forces
Forces that are equal in size and opposite in direction, resulting in a resultant force of zero.
Unbalanced forces
Forces that are unequal in size, resulting in a non-zero resultant force that changes motion.
Moment
The turning effect of a force, calculated as the force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the pivot.
Pivot
The fixed point about which an object turns or rotates.

أمثلة محلولة

1A mechanic applies a force of 40 N to the end of a spanner. The spanner is 0.25 m long from the pivot point on the bolt. Calculate the moment of the force.

اعرض الحل
  1. Identify the given values: force equals 40 N, distance equals 0.25 m.
  2. State the formula for a moment: moment equals force multiplied by distance.
  3. Substitute the values into the formula: moment equals 40 multiplied by 0.25.
  4. Calculate the final answer: moment equals 10 Nm.
  5. State the final answer with correct units: 10 Nm.

2A child of weight 300 N sits on a seesaw at a distance of 1.5 m from the pivot. At what distance from the pivot must an adult weighing 600 N sit to balance the seesaw?

اعرض الحل
  1. Identify the clockwise side and anticlockwise side to establish equilibrium.
  2. Use the principle of moments: clockwise moment equals anticlockwise moment.
  3. Calculate the known moment: 300 N multiplied by 1.5 m equals 450 Nm.
  4. Set the adult's moment equal to 450 Nm: 600 N multiplied by distance equals 450 Nm.
  5. Rearrange to find the distance: distance equals 450 divided by 600.
  6. Calculate the final answer: distance equals 0.75 m.

مفاهيم خاطئة شائعة

خطأ شائع An object needs a continuous force acting on it to keep moving at a constant speed.

في الحقيقة According to the laws of motion, once an object is moving, it will maintain a constant speed in a straight line unless acted upon by an unbalanced resistive force such as friction.

خطأ شائع If an object is stationary, there are no forces acting on it.

في الحقيقة A stationary object experiences balanced forces, such as gravity pulling downwards and a surface pushing upwards with equal magnitude.

خطأ شائع Heavy objects always fall faster because gravity pulls harder on them.

في الحقيقة In the absence of air resistance, all objects accelerate downwards at the exact same rate regardless of mass, because gravitational acceleration is constant for a given gravitational field.

خطأ شائع Turning a heavy bolt is easier if the force is applied closer to the nut.

في الحقيقة The turning effect is greater when the distance from the pivot is larger, so applying force further away from the nut requires less effort.
