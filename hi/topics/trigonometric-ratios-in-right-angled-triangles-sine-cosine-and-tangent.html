व्याख्या

Naming the Sides of a Right-Angled Triangle

Trigonometry is the branch of mathematics that studies relationships between side lengths and angles of triangles. This article focuses exclusively on right-angled triangles, which are triangles containing one interior angle equal to 90 degrees. Before calculating any trigonometric values, one must accurately name the three sides of a right-angled triangle relative to a chosen reference angle. The reference angle is always one of the two acute interior angles, meaning an angle strictly between 0 degrees and 90 degrees.

Every right-angled triangle has three distinct sides with specific names:

  • Hypotenuse: This is the longest side of the right-angled triangle. It is always located directly opposite the 90-degree right angle. Because it is opposite the largest angle in the triangle, it is always opposite the right angle and never changes its position regardless of which acute angle is chosen as the reference.
  • Opposite side: This is the side that lies directly across the triangle from the chosen reference angle. It does not touch the vertex of the reference angle.
  • Adjacent side: This is the side that sits next to the chosen reference angle, forming one of the arms of that angle along with the hypotenuse.

Consider a right-angled triangle ABC, where angle ABC is 90 degrees. Vertex B holds the right angle. The sides of the triangle are line segment AB, line segment BC, and line segment AC. Line segment AC is opposite the right angle at vertex B, so line segment AC is the hypotenuse. If the reference angle is chosen as angle BCA, then the opposite side is line segment AB because line segment AB lies across from angle BCA. The adjacent side is line segment BC because line segment BC touches vertex C and forms angle BCA. If the reference angle is instead chosen as angle BAC, the opposite side becomes line segment BC, and the adjacent side becomes line segment AB. The hypotenuse line segment AC remains unchanged.

Correct identification of these three sides is an absolute prerequisite for working with trigonometric ratios. Labelling must occur before writing any formula or performing any calculation. An error in identifying the opposite and adjacent sides leads to using the wrong ratio, resulting in an incorrect answer.

Defining Sine, Cosine and Tangent

Once the hypotenuse, opposite side, and adjacent side are correctly identified relative to a reference angle, these side lengths can be compared using division. In any right-angled triangle, the ratio of any two side lengths depends entirely on the size of the acute reference angle, not on the overall size of the triangle. Similar right-angled triangles share identical angle measurements and therefore share identical side length ratios. These fundamental ratios are given specific names: sine, cosine, and tangent.

The three primary trigonometric ratios are defined as follows:

  • Sine (sin): The ratio of the length of the opposite side to the length of the hypotenuse. Written as a formula, sin(angle) = opposite / hypotenuse.
  • Cosine (cos): The ratio of the length of the adjacent side to the length of the hypotenuse. Written as a formula, cos(angle) = adjacent / hypotenuse.
  • Tangent (tan): The ratio of the length of the opposite side to the length of the adjacent side. Written as a formula, tan(angle) = opposite / adjacent.

A widely used mnemonic device to remember these definitions is the acronym SOH CAH TOA. This stands for:

  • SOH: Sin = Opposite / Hypotenuse
  • CAH: Cos = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
  • TOA: Tan = Opposite / Adjacent

When applying these formulas, angle represents the measure of the chosen acute reference angle in degrees. The value of the sine and cosine of an acute angle is always a positive number strictly between 0 and 1, because the hypotenuse is strictly longer than either the opposite or the adjacent side. The value of the tangent of an acute angle can be any positive number, as the opposite side can be shorter than, equal to, or longer than the adjacent side depending on the angle size. If the angle approaches 0 degrees, the opposite side approaches 0, making sine and tangent approach 0 while cosine approaches 1. If the angle approaches 90 degrees, the adjacent side approaches 0, making cosine approach 0 while sine approaches 1 and tangent grows larger without bound.

Finding a Missing Side

Trigonometric ratios allow the calculation of an unknown side length in a right-angled triangle when one acute angle and one side length are known. The process requires a systematic approach to ensure the correct ratio is selected.

The steps to find a missing side are:

  1. Label the three sides of the triangle as hypotenuse, opposite, and adjacent relative to the known acute angle.
  2. Identify which side length is known and which side length is unknown (the one to be found).
  3. Select the trigonometric ratio that connects the known side, the unknown side, and the known angle using SOH CAH TOA.
  4. Substitute the known values into the chosen trigonometric equation.
  5. Rearrange the equation algebraically to isolate the unknown variable.
  6. Evaluate the expression using a scientific calculator and state the final answer with appropriate units.

When the unknown side is in the numerator of the fraction, the solution requires multiplication. When the unknown side is in the denominator of the fraction, the solution requires division by the trigonometric ratio of the angle. Every calculation must maintain adequate decimal precision during intermediate steps, rounding only at the final stage of working.

Finding a Missing Angle

Trigonometric ratios also allow the calculation of an unknown interior acute angle in a right-angled triangle when any two side lengths are known. Finding an angle requires the use of inverse trigonometric functions, written as sin-1, cos-1, and tan-1, which are read as inverse sine, inverse cosine, and inverse tangent.

The steps to find a missing angle are:

  1. Label the three sides of the triangle as hypotenuse, opposite, and adjacent relative to the unknown angle, which is designated by a variable such as x.
  2. Identify the two side lengths whose numerical values are given in the diagram.
  3. Select the trigonometric ratio that connects those two known sides using SOH CAH TOA.
  4. Set up the equation, such as sin(x) = opposite / hypotenuse.
  5. Apply the corresponding inverse trigonometric function to both sides to isolate the angle x, such as x = sin-1(opposite / hypotenuse).
  6. Evaluate the expression using a scientific calculator set to degree mode, and state the final answer in degrees.

Inverse trigonometric functions perform the reverse operation of standard trigonometric ratios. If sin(30 degrees) = 0.5, then sin-1(0.5) = 30 degrees. The calculator requires pressing a shift or second-function key combined with the sin, cos, or tan button to activate the inverse functions.

Common Mistakes and Misconceptions

Learners encountering trigonometry for the first time frequently encounter specific pitfalls. Being aware of these errors prevents incorrect calculations.

A very common error is failing to identify the correct reference angle, which leads to swapping the opposite and adjacent sides. Because the opposite and adjacent sides depend entirely on which angle is chosen, labelling must always start by locating the reference angle before choosing between SOH, CAH, and TOA.

Another frequent mistake involves calculator settings. Scientific calculators operate in different angle units, including degrees, radians, and grads. If a calculator is accidentally set to radians, the calculated trigonometric values will be completely wrong for geometry problems measured in degrees. Students must verify that the calculator display shows a small letter D or the word DEG before beginning any work.

Algebraic errors occur when solving equations where the unknown side is in the denominator. For example, if cos(40 degrees) = 12 / x, learners sometimes mistakenly multiply both sides by 40 degrees or divide 12 by cos(40 degrees) incorrectly. The correct algebraic rearrangement multiplies both sides by x to get x multiplied by cos(40 degrees) = 12, and then divides both sides by cos(40 degrees) to get x = 12 / cos(40 degrees).

Finally, learners often round intermediate decimal values too early in multi-step calculations. Rounding sin(35 degrees) to 0.57 instead of using the full calculator decimal expansion introduces rounding errors that accumulate by the final answer. The rule is to keep all digits in the calculator memory and round only the final reported value.

हल किए गए उदाहरण

1In triangle PQR, angle PQR is 90 degrees, angle PRQ is 35 degrees, and side QR is 12 cm. Calculate the length of the hypotenuse PR.

हल दिखाएँ
  1. Identify the given information in triangle PQR: angle PQR = 90 degrees, reference angle PRQ = 35 degrees, and side QR = 12 cm.
  2. Label the sides relative to the reference angle 35 degrees at vertex R: side PQ is opposite angle PRQ, side PR is the hypotenuse opposite the 90-degree angle at Q, and side QR is adjacent to angle PRQ.
  3. Determine which sides are involved: side QR (12 cm) is the adjacent side, and side PR (let us call its length x) is the hypotenuse.
  4. Select the trigonometric ratio that connects adjacent and hypotenuse: cosine, because cos = adjacent / hypotenuse (CAH).
  5. Set up the equation: cos(35 degrees) = 12 / x.
  6. Rearrange the equation to isolate x. Multiply both sides by x: x multiplied by cos(35 degrees) = 12.
  7. Divide both sides by cos(35 degrees): x = 12 / cos(35 degrees).
  8. Evaluate using a scientific calculator: cos(35 degrees) is approximately 0.819152.
  9. Calculate x = 12 / 0.819152 = 14.6487...
  10. Round to one decimal place: x = 14.6 cm.
  11. State the final answer: The length of the hypotenuse PR is 14.6 cm.

2In triangle XYZ, angle XYZ is 90 degrees, side XY is 7 cm, and side YZ is 10 cm. Calculate the size of angle YZX.

हल दिखाएँ
  1. Identify the given information in triangle XYZ: angle XYZ = 90 degrees, side XY = 7 cm, and side YZ = 10 cm.
  2. Let angle YZX be represented by the variable x degrees as the reference angle.
  3. Label the sides relative to reference angle x: side XY is opposite angle x, side XZ is the hypotenuse opposite the 90-degree angle at Y, and side YZ is adjacent to angle x.
  4. Determine which side lengths are known: side XY = 7 cm (opposite) and side YZ = 10 cm (adjacent).
  5. Select the trigonometric ratio that connects opposite and adjacent: tangent, because tan = opposite / adjacent (TOA).
  6. Set up the equation: tan(x) = 7 / 10.
  7. Calculate the fraction: 7 / 10 = 0.7.
  8. Apply the inverse tangent function to isolate x: x = tan-1(0.7).
  9. Evaluate using a scientific calculator in degree mode: x = 34.9920...
  10. Round to one decimal place: x = 35.0 degrees.
  11. State the final answer: The size of angle YZX is 35.0 degrees.
